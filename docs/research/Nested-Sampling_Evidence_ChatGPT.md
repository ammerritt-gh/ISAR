# Nested-sampling evidence for peak counting (research report)

> **Status:** live
> **Kind:** external research report the operator obtained (2026-10-25), kept as received; not maintained with the code

**MY INFERENCE — Your current evidences are not calibrated well enough for peak counting.** The 5.2-nat difference is \\(5.2/\\sqrt{0.8^2+0.8^2}=4.6\\) nominal combined standard deviations. It demonstrates inconsistency with the reported uncertainty model; a reference calculation is still needed to establish the signed bias.

**S — The useful scaling relations describe ideal sampling error and cost:**

\\[ H=D\_{\\rm KL}(p\\Vert\\pi)=\\mathbb E_p[\\ln L]-\\ln Z,\\qquad n\_{\\rm bulk}\\approx N\_{\\rm live}H,\\qquad \\sigma\_{\\ln Z}\\approx\\sqrt{\\frac{H}{N\_{\\rm live}}}. \\]

At fixed desired precision, these imply \\(N\_{\\rm live}\\propto H\\) and roughly \\(H^2\\) replacements. They do not bound implementation bias from inadequate constrained-prior sampling. R6 §1.5, Supplement D. [arxiv.org](http://arxiv.org)

**I — Terminal \\(-\\ln X\\approx100\\) does not establish \\(H=100\\).** Even if \\(H=100\\), the ideal uncertainty scale at 400 live points is about **0.5 nats**. Large compression alone therefore does not explain your multi-nat discrepancies.

**S — Dimension is a particularly weak prescription for chain length.** Higson et al. found approximately 10 repeats sufficient for their 10D Gaussian, whereas their challenging **10D LogGamma mixture required more than \\(10^3\\)** repeats to make implementation effects a small fraction of total error. R3 §7.1. [arxiv.org](http://arxiv.org)

**I — Severe discrepancies are quantitatively plausible, although your specific magnitude remains unproven.** Fowlie et al.’s Table 2 implies offsets of **−6.39 nats at 20D** and **+48.37 nats at 30D**, each with mean reported uncertainty approximately **0.64 nats**, using 1,000 live points and only one slice repeat. This is strong precedent for failure, but not a calibrated prediction of 50 nats at 11–14 dimensions.

## 3. Protocol for a trustworthy reference and uncertainty

1. **I — Freeze and audit the integral.** Use identical likelihood constants, data, prior transform and numerical conventions across engines. Check deterministic likelihood evaluations. Sorting \\(k\\) independent uniform energies on a common interval already induces density \\(k!/\\Delta E^k\\) on the ordered region: add no further factorial correction. Preserve original unit-cube samples; sorting is many-to-one and can complicate computational geometry.
2. **I — Validate against known integrals at relevant difficulty.** Use 15D Gaussian and separated-mixture targets, including compression comparable to the real run, plus a curved target with independently known normalization. Verify bias and uncertainty coverage over repeated seeds. Passing a tight Gaussian alone is insufficient reassurance for your spectra.
3. **I — Converge the real target along separate controls.** First perform the factorial screen in §5. Then increase replacement length until the mean stabilizes, and increase live points independently. Choose a reference tolerance beforehand—for example **0.25 nats**—and gather enough repeats to resolve shifts of that size.
4. **S — Use complementary diagnostics.** Insertion ranks should be uniform; test the whole run and windows of approximately \\(N\_{\\rm live}\\) iterations, with appropriate discrete-null and multiple-testing calibration. Passing does not certify correctness: Fowlie et al. report false negatives. Bootstrap **threads**, preserving nested-sampling structure, rather than arbitrary dead points. R4 §3, Table 2; R2 §§4.1–4.2. [arxiv.org](http://arxiv.org)
5. **I — Obtain an independent cold calculation.** Use UltraNest or PolyChord with the original prior. For PolyChord, an initial **1,000–2,000 live points and** `num_repeats=5d`, followed by doubling checks, is reasonable. UltraNest also recommends checking increased step counts. If engines disagree, use tempered SMC or thermodynamic integration to test a different exploration/normalization procedure. R7 §§5.2, 5.7; R15 FAQ. [arxiv.org](http://arxiv.org)
6. **I — Calibrate uncertainty with approximately 8–10 independent runs at the accepted settings.** Record individual results, not only their mean: missed-mode failures can produce non-Gaussian scatter.

**S — Higson’s diagnostic decomposition**, assuming uncorrelated stochastic and implementation contributions, is

\\[ \\widehat{\\sigma}\_{\\rm extra} =\\sqrt{\\max\\!\\left(0,s\_{\\rm runs}^{2} -\\overline{\\sigma\_{\\rm thread}^{2}}\\right)}. \\]

Repeated-run scatter still misses shared systematic bias. R3 §5, Eqs. (6)–(7). [arxiv.org](http://arxiv.org)

**I — Report three quantities separately:** empirical run scatter; Monte Carlo uncertainty of the combined estimate; and the remaining sensitivity to stricter settings/independent methods. The last is a sensitivity range, not a guaranteed confidence bound. Merge validated NS runs; for unbiased SMC/importance estimates, average **\\(Z\\)** using log-sum-exp before taking the logarithm. Averaging cannot remove shared missed mass.

## 4. Warm starts and alternatives

**S — Posterior repartitioning preserves the integral through**

\\[ q=(1-\\alpha)q\_{\\rm fit}+\\alpha\\pi,\\qquad \\widetilde L=L\\frac{\\pi}{q},\\qquad \\int q\\widetilde L\\,d\\theta=Z, \\]

provided densities and support are handled correctly. Chen et al. give the general identity; SuperNest gives the additive mixture explicitly. **Powering an already uniform prior produces no useful change.** R12 §4.1, Eqs. (6)–(9); R13 §4; R14 Eq. (13). [Springer Nature Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11222-018-9841-3?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**I — Operational conditions:** fit \\(q\\) in a pilot and freeze it; include normalization, truncation and Jacobian factors; initialize with actual draws from \\(q\\); constrain on \\(\\widetilde L\\). Merely seeding ordinary NS with posterior samples is invalid. Although \\(\\alpha&gt;0\\) ensures \\(\\pi/q\\le1/\\alpha\\), a narrow missed mode may remain practically undiscovered. “Exact” here describes the target integral, not an unbiased finite-run \\(\\ln Z\\).

**S — Salomone’s guarantee needs careful attribution.** The final 2025 paper proves unbiasedness for **NS-SMC with fixed thresholds**; its adaptive variant is consistent under conditions but not generally unbiased. These are not blanket guarantees for ordinary NS using finite MCMC replacements. R11 Proposition 1, Table 1. [academic.oup.com](http://academic.oup.com)

**I — For your inexpensive likelihood, I would test a cheap mixture proposal only after establishing the cold reference.** A Laplace approximation used to construct \\(q\\) need not approximate the integral; the exact importance weights correct it. Include proposal evaluation/training overhead in wall-clock comparisons, and validate with independently fitted proposals and fresh estimation batches.

## 5. Direct diagnosis and fastest discriminating experiment

**I — My leading explanation is the combination of local-walk under-exploration and curved/multimodal geometry, with population-dependent effects particularly plausible at 128 live points.** Large prior compression raises the computational burden and can exacerbate these problems; it is not a standalone explanation for a 5–50-nat bias. The tighter-prior toy changes both compression and accessible geometry, so it does not isolate the cause. Identical best likelihoods provide little reassurance about integrated volume.

**I — The fastest useful discriminating experiment is a small \\(2\\times2\\) factorial screen on one problematic spectrum/model:**

\\[ N\_{\\rm live}\\in\\{128,400\\},\\qquad m\\in\\{d+20,\\;4(d+20)\\}, \\]

with **at least three independent seeds per cell**, identical priors/likelihood, `dlogz=0.05`, and `maxiter=None`.


| Result **(I)**                                                     | Interpretation                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Longer walks move the mean and reduce disagreement                 | Strong evidence for inadequate replacement exploration                               |
| Live-point dependence remains after replacement-length convergence | Mode resolution or other population-dependent sampling effects; inspect mode weights |
| Both controls matter                                               | Combined failure                                                                     |
| Neither produces a clear change                                    | Inconclusive: quadrupling steps is a perturbation, not a convergence certificate     |


**I — Three seeds are enough for an initial screen of discrepancies this large, but insufficient for a precise uncertainty estimate.** Follow the successful direction to a plateau, then perform the reference protocol. I would defer prior tuning until this calculation is stable.
