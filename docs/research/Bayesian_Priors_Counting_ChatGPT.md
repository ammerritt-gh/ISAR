## 2. Q2 — Amplitude

### Height versus area

**[I]** Define \\(H\\) as the maximum expected count contribution of one component **after your measured resolution convolution**, at a reference exposure and the existing binning. For the unchanged model, compute the height \\(q(E_0,\\Gamma,T)\\) of a unit-amplitude component:

\\[ H=qA,\\qquad p_A(A\\mid E_0,\\Gamma,T)=q\\,p_H(qA). \\]

**[I]** Exact evidence is invariant under this change of coordinates **when the Jacobian is included**. Independent flat priors on height and area are different scientific assumptions. Include the resulting conditional prior and its derivatives in the Laplace calculation.

**[I]** For a normalized, unconvolved Lorentzian, \\(A=\\pi\\Gamma H\\). Away from prior boundaries, at equal height uncertainty \\(\\sigma_H\\), a flat-area amplitude factor scales as

\\[ \\frac{\\sqrt{2\\pi}\\sigma_A}{A\_{\\max}} =\\frac{\\sqrt{2\\pi}\\pi\\Gamma\\sigma_H}{A\_{\\max}}. \\]

Thus flat area relatively favors broader peaks compared with flat height. Likelihood, correlations and boundary clipping can outweigh this local effect. Use your actual convolved DHO conversion \\(q\\), rather than this Lorentzian shortcut.

### Ceiling dependence

**[S]** Evidence under a uniform prior contains the inverse prior-volume normalization; diluting that prior decreases evidence when the additional volume contributes negligible likelihood. See Llorente et al., §4.3.1 and Appendix A. [arxiv.org](http://arxiv.org)

**[I]** Consequently, for \\(A\_{\\max}\\rightarrow F A\_{\\max}\\), with the same change applied independently to every peak,

\\[ \\boxed{\\Delta\\ln Z_k=-k\\ln F,\\qquad \\Delta\\ln B\_{k+1,k}=-\\ln F.} \\]

**[I]** Shrinking by 2,000 therefore increases the additional-peak log Bayes factor by **7.601 nats**. The literal ratio \\(1.5\\times10^6/900\\) gives **7.419 nats**. These results require negligible likelihood truncation and unchanged prior shape.

### Using the spectrum to set its own range

**[S]** Both Sivia’s QENS prescription and the tutorial permit current-data intensity information; the tutorial explicitly prefers previous measurements/theory but acknowledges practical inspection of current data. Moores et al. explicitly use the observed intensity range. [bayes.wustl.edu](http://bayes.wustl.edu)

**[I]** Your independence principle is therefore stricter than some published practice. Nevertheless, inserting \\(p(\\theta;D)\\) into an ordinary evidence integral generally loses the interpretation of a fixed, normalized prior-predictive distribution over possible datasets. It should be disclosed as empirical Bayes or an exploratory calibration.

**[I]** There is **no universal correction in nats** for choosing \\(H\_{\\max}=\\max y\\). For example, changing a fixed ceiling from 3,000 to 900 gives \\(+\\ln(3000/900)=1.204\\) nats per additional peak only if neither ceiling clips relevant likelihood mass. A ceiling at the observed maximum can clip uncertainty in the strongest component.

**[I]** Gregory’s reported odds correspond to a **2.394-nat** change from flat to log-uniform amplitude priors with identical bounds. This quantifies prior-form sensitivity, rather than data-reuse bias. Dudbridge’s \\(d/2\\) result concerns a different, specified reuse procedure and cannot be transferred to your maximum-count rule. [ib.edu.ar](http://ib.edu.ar)

### Alternatives

**[I]** Proper half-Cauchy, exponential or lognormal priors remove a hard upper ceiling, but retain a scale that needs justification. Independent historical spectra, reference measurements or a proper hierarchy with integrated hyperparameters can supply it. Monitor exposure, resolution and window alone do not determine sample scattering intensity.

**[I]** A bounded log-uniform prior reduces upper-bound sensitivity to

\\[ \\Delta\\ln B= -\\ln\\!\\left[ \\frac{\\ln(F A\_{\\max}/A\_{\\min})} {\\ln(A\_{\\max}/A\_{\\min})} \\right], \\]

but introduces a positive lower bound. \\(1/A\\) is improper at zero. Also, “scale-invariant” is not automatically the Poisson Jeffreys prior: for a fixed shape with zero other intensity, the latter is proportional to \\(A^{-1/2}\\).

## 3. Q3 — Width

**[I]** The literature does not establish a universal choice: Sivia uses flat widths, Allison log-uniform widths, and De Francesco hierarchical lognormal dampings. These encode different beliefs about scale.

**[I]** Resolution does not impose a physical minimum intrinsic \\(\\Gamma\\). When the likelihood becomes nearly constant below some \\(\\Gamma_r\\), that region contributes approximately

\\[ L_0\\,P(\\Gamma&lt;\\Gamma_r), \\]

rather than a well-measured Gaussian width factor. For log-uniform widths this mass is

\\[ \\frac{\\ln(\\Gamma_r/\\Gamma\_{\\min})} {\\ln(\\Gamma\_{\\max}/\\Gamma\_{\\min})}, \\]

so the arbitrary lower cutoff matters. Neither bin spacing nor \\(R/100\\) establishes an identifiable-width threshold; that also depends on signal strength and overlap.

**[I]** A prior finite at zero avoids a singular concentration there. A numerical lower cutoff should be justified by convergence to the resolution-limited prediction.

**[I]** For your denominator, the DHO poles become overdamped when \\(\\Gamma&gt;E_0\\); do not impose \\(\\Gamma&lt;E_0\\). In strong overdamping, the slow rate approaches \\(E_0^2/(2\\Gamma)\\), producing degeneracy with the central response. Neither resolution nor the measurement window gives a universal physical upper bound. A finite upper bound is a declared modeling judgment that should be tested.

## 4. Q4 — Positions and ordering

**[S]** De Francesco explicitly normalizes the ordered frequencies using \\(k!\\); Sivia’s tutorial accounts for equivalent labeled maxima. [Academia](https://www.academia.edu/113571249/Bayesian_approach_to_the_analysis_of_neutron_Brillouin_scattering_data_on_liquid_metals?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**[I]** For exchangeable peaks independently uniform over \\(0…W\\), the normalized ordered prior is

\\[ p(E_1,\\ldots,E_k\\mid k)= \\frac{k!}{W^k}\\, \\mathbf1(0&lt;E_1&lt;\\cdots&lt;E_k&lt;W). \\]

**[I]** Integrating over the full labeled space already includes all equivalent modes. Integrating over the ordered region requires the density above. Omitting \\(k!\\) lowers \\(\\ln Z_k\\) by \\(\\ln k!\\), adding a spurious **\\(\\ln(k+1)\\)** penalty for the next peak: **1.609 nats for \\(4\\rightarrow5\\)**.

**[I]** Drawing iid uniform energies and sorting implements the normalized ordered distribution automatically; do not add another factorial. Sequential draws \\(E_1\\sim U(0,W)\\), \\(E_2\\sim U(E_1,W)\\), etc. produce a different prior. Minimum-separation constraints also constitute additional prior information.

## 5. Q5 — A concrete recipe and worked penalty

## 6. Open uncertainties and limits of the check

- **[I] Independent intensity calibration remains missing.** A smaller arbitrary scale can change the answer without becoming better justified. The De Francesco numerical hyperparameters and the inaccessible von der Linden chapters remain unresolved literature gaps.
- **[I] Laplace accuracy is a separate uncertainty.** At \\(H\\simeq0\\), position and damping become unidentified; near \\(\\Gamma=0\\), overlapping modes or multiple posterior maxima, the Gaussian volume approximation can fail. Prior sweeps cannot validate that approximation. Nested sampling can address integration accuracy while retaining prior dependence.
- **[I] Confirm amplitude normalization precisely:** full versus window-integrated area, detailed-balance weighting, binning and exposure convention determine \\(q\\).
- **[I] Four settings do not exhaust prior uncertainty:** they leave amplitude functional form and the width knee largely untested.
- **[I] Stability of selected \\(k\\) cannot establish a physical mode count by itself.** Finite-window nonidentifiability, resolution-tail uncertainty and inadequacy of a fixed spectral description can persist across all tested priors.
