# The statistics behind an ISAR results row

> **Status:** live
> **Authority:** canonical for statistical methods; §7 is the sole record of the 2026-07-19 audit
> **Verified against code revision:** `1310969` (2026-07-30)
> **Role:** the statistics design doc (design/-equivalent; kept at this path because frozen evidence reports cite it)

*For physicists who fit by hand — and who will check.*

Every fitted number ISAR reports answers one of three questions. **Minimize:** given a model,
which parameters do the counts support? ISAR minimizes the Baker–Cousins Poisson likelihood
rather than a χ², because at triage-level counts the standard χ² choices are not approximately
right — they are measurably biased, in opposite directions, by an exact identity (Fig. 2).
**Select:** which model? A posterior over structures from per-candidate Bayesian evidence
(Laplace, with escalation to nested sampling), Akaike weights persisting as a recorded
diagnostic, and a fork reported when the data genuinely cannot decide (Fig. 3).
**Certify:** what uncertainty? A ladder of intervals — Hessian, profile,
parametric bootstrap — whose 68% coverage we measured on known truth rather than assumed
(Figs. 4–6), together with explicit flags for the regime where every rung fails at once (Fig. 7).

None of this is asked on faith. Every figure here is generated by one seeded script,
[docs/stats/make_figures.py](stats/make_figures.py) (SEED = 20260710), against either synthetic
ensembles or the archived workspace `mvp_zone_test16`, and every chapter ends with the exact
command that regenerates its evidence. Where a capability is not yet wired in, it is tagged
**[Planned]** — never implied.

## 0. Three questions, one vocabulary

Every number in a `results.csv` row answers exactly one of the three questions, and trusting a
number means trusting the machinery behind its question. Fig. 1 is the map; note the one dashed
box — the single-fit Bayesian rung of the certification ladder — which is built but **does not
certify on its own**.

![FIG. 1](stats/figures/three_questions.svg)

**FIG. 1.** The pipeline map: minimize → select → certify. Generated by
`docs/stats/make_figures.py` (no data inputs).

One founding priority explains this document's proportions: **error estimation is the spine,
not one of four ribs**. The uncertainty is what the human acts on, what computed confidence is
built from, and — the part that matters most — the entire input to autonomous steering ("put
beam where the error is too big"). A wrong error bar does not just mislabel one scan; it
misdirects every future-measurement decision built on top of it. That is why certification gets
the largest share of the machinery, and why its calibration is measured rather than assumed
(§3).

Vocabulary is discipline, not pedagogy, in this document:

> - **Bootstrap** means *frequentist-by-simulation*: refit many Poisson redraws of the best-fit
>   model and read the spread. It is not Bayesian and is never called "credible".
> - The branch-assignment weights (`branch_posterior`, `assignment_entropy`, `branch_id`) are a
>   normalized, versioned assignment-score distribution **[Implemented]**. They are probability-like
>   weights, but are not claimed to be a calibrated Bayesian posterior.
> - The dispersion-prior MAP fit (`prior_omega`, `prior_sigma` → `fit_kind=soft_prior`) is a
>   prior-regularized point estimate **[Implemented]**. It is not a stored posterior distribution
>   or a credible interval.
> - Bayesian credible **intervals** on ω₀ are **[Implemented, diagnostics-only]**: the nested
>   sampler (`isar/analyze/errors/bayes.py`) is wired through `--errors bayes` and stores
>   `bayes_interval` plus posterior samples and agreement diagnostics. A physical row never
>   certifies directly from `bayes_interval`. A pooled master's legacy model interval can,
>   however, include those nested particles in a model-dependent mixture and currently seats
>   certification; §4.1 records that distinct authority and its limitations.
> - `confidence` is a heuristic triage score, not an empirical or Bayesian probability.

### 0.1 Additive statistical-semantics contract

STAT-1 adds optional metadata without changing a fit, score, interval, verdict, stop, or action.
Full records live in `session.json`; compact projections are appended to `results.csv`; legacy
rows expose no semantics rather than inventing producer provenance. The four record schemas are
`isar.fit_selection_semantics/2` (which replaced `/1` outright when evidence selection landed —
H1/H4; `/1` records are unreadable-legacy by design), `isar.interval_semantics/1`,
`isar.probability_semantics/1`, and `isar.predictive_semantics/1`.

Every authority declaration is scoped: for example, an interval can remain active for
`legacy_master_certification` and `stopping` while being ineligible for `eig_shadow`,
`eig_authoritative`, and `acquisition_selection`. `unknown_legacy` is reader-only. If a producer
cannot construct valid metadata, it records a structured `semantics_construction_failed` warning
and publishes the otherwise-valid scientific result with semantics unavailable.

Candidate comparability is represented by four identities, not inferred from a shared model label:
the exact data/energy-grid/mask/normalization/likelihood context, model parameterization, effective
constraints, and their combined comparison context. Model-set fingerprints contain sorted
structured model/context records, so enumeration order is irrelevant, while changing a candidate
route or Akaike denominator changes the complete selection identity. The selection record publishes
its aggregate eligibility and every candidate-specific reason. STAT-2a consumes those
producer-owned classifications in a diagnostics-only shadow comparison rather than re-deriving
them; production selection enforces them for authority now, since evidence selection is the
production selector (H4) — a recorded AIC fallback lane is a named degraded mode and is
selection-ineligible for acquisition steering.

For intervals, producer capability and realized authority are separate. A bootstrap or profile
producer may offer an interval to the delivery ladder, but only the value actually selected after
reliability/trust gates, branch triage, and connected tracking carries delivery or stopping
authority. Predictive/projection semantics fingerprints are authenticated by readers. Missing
semantics remain valid legacy numerical input; present-but-modified semantics are refused under the
stable `predictive_semantics_invalid` reason.

## 1. Minimize: why not the χ² you already use

The default everywhere — Igor, PAN, home-grown Matlab — is least squares with σ² = data
(Neyman) or σ² = model (Pearson). At high counts both are fine. The problem at low counts is
not approximate, it is an identity: for a constant rate the three costs have exact closed-form
minimizers — the **harmonic mean** of the counts (Neyman), the **RMS** (modified Pearson), and
the **arithmetic mean** (Baker–Cousins Poisson likelihood). The two χ²'s provably straddle the
likelihood answer; Fig. 2(a) shows the three cost curves and their marked minimizers on a single
seeded Poisson draw. This is not a simulation artifact — it is what those cost functions *are*.

The measured consequence on the actual lineshape is Fig. 2(b): N = 500 synthetic phonon scans
per count level, the *same data* fit three times with only the estimator swapped. At 5 expected
peak counts Neyman pulls the fitted amplitude down by 38.8% and modified Pearson pushes it up
by 25.3%, while Baker–Cousins sits at −5.9%; all three converge by ~100 counts. If your
intuition was trained on well-counted scans this is quite a surprise — and a phonon's Γ and its
Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio are exactly the quantities these biases distort, at exactly the count
rates where triage decisions get made.

### 1.1 How the minimum is found: the amplitudes are profiled out

**[Implemented, 2026-10-23]** Every amplitude of the model (each mode's height `h`, the elastic `h`,
the quasi-elastic `QE`, the background `BG`) enters the mean linearly, and non-negatively. So for
given nonlinear parameters (each mode's `omega0` = E0 and `Gamma`) the best amplitudes are a convex
problem with one answer. The Baker–Cousins cost is minimised over them inside their recipe
bounds (§2.1) by a Poisson non-negative least-squares solve: Newton steps on the cost itself,
each kept inside the box, warm-started from the previous evaluation (`backends.poisson_nnls`).
MIGRAD then sees only the nonlinear parameters, and the cost it minimises is the *profile*
`min over h of C(omega0, Gamma, h)`. A zero-count point still contributes its mean only (no
floor, convention D3), and the anti-Stokes side is still the derived Bose partner inside each
mode's unit shape, never a free amplitude. An amplitude with a soft prior on it, or a hard-fixed
one, is not profiled: it stays with MIGRAD or at its value. The iminuit fit is the profiled one;
the comparison backends (scipy, lmfit), the bootstrap and profile-likelihood refits and the MAP
refit of §2 still fit every parameter. When MIGRAD on the profile fails (a start with a mode's
amplitude on zero makes the profile exactly flat along that mode), the free fit of every
parameter from the same start is the result.

The optimum of the profile is the joint optimum, so nothing downstream changes meaning. The
reported covariance, and the curvature the Laplace evidence reads, is **one HESSE over every free
parameter at the joint optimum, on the full cost**, not the profile's curvature; the evidence is
therefore the same integral over the same parameters as before. Two details of that HESSE:

- An interior amplitude is unbounded in it. At the optimum Minuit's limit transform cancels out
  of the covariance exactly, but numerically a freshly started Minuit differentiates an amplitude
  near the bottom of its million-count range badly: on rung-1 models it was 20–30% off a
  finite-difference Hessian in some sigmas, and the plain curvature is within 0.2%.
- An amplitude the solve put exactly on a bound is held there, so its variance is zero. It is
  pinned, and the evidence ladder treats a pin by its own rules (the degenerate-limit rule of §2
  for a mode's `A` or `E` at zero, escalation for a pinned `BG`). A free fit only ever approached
  such a bound, and its HESSE gave the amplitude a variance the limit transform had squashed
  towards zero.

Nested sampling is unchanged: it samples every parameter, amplitudes included, through the same
prior transform. Integrating the amplitudes out analytically is not done.

Two consequences are deliberate. Because the solve puts a vanishing amplitude exactly on zero,
the degenerate-limit rule now fires there where a free fit stopped a little above zero; and a fit
that previously ran into MIGRAD's call budget crawling along an amplitude direction now
converges, so the budget's weak regularising effect (`backends._migrad_ncall`) acts only on the
nonlinear parameters. The resolution kernel's Fourier transform is computed once per grid and
resolution (`lineshapes.ResolutionConvolver`), and all modes of a model share one convolution.
Measured on one BL43-like synthetic spectrum at ceiling 6, one worker: rung 1 took 319 s before
these changes and 25 s after, with 871k objective calls before and about 88k after.

## 2. Select: which model, decided by Bayesian evidence

Adding parameters always lowers the cost, so raw χ² (or raw likelihood) always votes for the
more flexible model, and eyeballing a shoulder is not reproducible. Since H4, ISAR decides by
**evidence**: each candidate model gets a marginal likelihood lnZ, and selection is the posterior
**P(s) = π_s·Z_s / Σ π·Z** **[Implemented]**. The Occam penalty is the integral itself, not a
parameter tax. Evidence is comparable only within one data-likelihood arena (the record carries the
arena fingerprint; cross-arena comparison raises), and priors enter exactly once (a transported soft
constraint IS its parameter's prior).

The **fast pass** (§2.2) prices every model one way **[Partial]**: a Laplace approximation at the
*posterior mode*, with the Gaussian cut to the prior's support (the box rule,
`prior.laplace_log_evidence`), and no sampling, bootstrap or profile interval. Its evidence contract is:

- **Coordinates.** A peak's amplitude coordinate is its convolved height h (§8), the maximum of its
  continuous convolved Stokes-side profile, so the flat height prior (§2.1) is a genuine prior on h.
  ω₀ and Γ run over 0 to 20 meV (ω₀ from 1e-3 meV). The area is derived from h, ω₀ and Γ.
- **Curvature.** The optimiser (bounded trust-region least squares on the signed Poisson-deviance
  residuals) finds the fit; the evidence is then taken at the posterior mode, likelihood times prior,
  with the finite-difference Hessian of minus (log-likelihood + log-prior) in the same coordinates:
  ln Z = ln L + ln prior + (d/2) ln 2π − ½ ln det H, and a coordinate pinned at a bound is handled by
  the box rule. The Gauss–Newton J^T J of the optimiser is not used for the evidence.
- **Prior weight.** Each count k has equal prior weight, shared equally over its V(k) elastic/background
  variants and, within a variant, over its 2^k delta/finite-width combinations (each peak equally likely
  to be a delta function or to have a width). The weight of one model is 1/(V(k)·2^k), normalised
  within the count, so a count's total does not grow with its number of masks.
- **Basins.** Distinct, well-separated solutions of one model add their local evidence; repeated starts
  that reach the same solution count once. Two solutions are the same basin when every mode's ω₀, Γ and
  h agree within a fraction of the resolution FWHM, of the larger width and of the larger height
  (`basin_merge_fwhm`, `basin_gamma_frac`, `basin_height_frac`).
- **What the probabilities are.** Approximate empirical-Bayes model probabilities under the stated
  priors (the height prior reads the spectrum's own counts, §2.1), recorded as such.

The nested-sampling lane of the earlier pipeline (dynesty at 128 live points for the count's
contenders, the validity ladder Laplace → MAP refit → sampling, and the dominance prune) is still in
the code and still decides counts until `scan_delivery.fit_one` calls the fast pass; the deep pass that
replaces it is a later job, and this section no longer describes its tuning.

**In plain terms**, because this paragraph earns its keep at the podium: the likelihood at the
best-fit point always improves when you add a knob, so it cannot judge between models. The
*evidence* (marginal likelihood, Z) asks a different question — not "how well does this model fit
at its best tuning?" but "how well does it fit *averaged over everything its parameters are
allowed to do*?" A model with an extra peak spreads its bets over a much larger parameter
volume; if the data does not need that peak, most of that volume fits badly and drags the
average down. That is the Occam penalty — not AIC's flat fee of 2 per parameter, but the
measured cost of the parameter volume the model wasted. The fast pass computes that average the
cheap way: treat the peak of likelihood × prior as a Gaussian bump and integrate it analytically
(Laplace — fine when the peak is clean), but cut the bump where the prior ends, since a width or
amplitude cannot be negative and a bump that spills past its bound would otherwise be priced as if
the spilled part were allowed. Where a Gaussian bump cannot be trusted at all (singular curvature),
the model has no evidence and the result says so (§2.2, the states). The selected structure is the
one holding the largest share of the total evidence.

When evidence cannot be computed at all, or the set is incomplete in that sense, selection falls
back to AIC and *says so* — the record names the lane (`aic_no_evidence_inputs` /
`aic_all_evidence_invalid` / `evidence_budget_exhausted`), and a fallback-lane record is
ineligible to steer acquisition.

**AIC** (= cost + 2 per free parameter) and **Akaike weights** are computed and persisted per
candidate as the diagnostics lane, plus a nested likelihood-ratio test for nested pairs
**[Implemented]**. An Akaike weight is neither a Bayesian posterior model probability nor a
Bayes factor — under `/2` the two live side by side in the candidate table, and the posterior is
the one that drives. STAT-1 records estimator, effective constraints, candidate route, selection
eligibility, and each model-set transition.

STAT-2a now also records a diagnostics-only `isar.model_selection/2-shadow` comparison
**[Implemented]**. For an unconstrained complete route it reuses the exact fitted candidate objects;
when delivery constraints are effective it fits the complete standard family under the same data,
mask, normalization, likelihood, and unconstrained parameterization. The full record is persisted
in `session.json` and a compact projection is appended to `results.csv`. A finite runtime allowance
skips the shadow work with a structured reason, and any shadow failure leaves the legacy selector,
verdict, intervals, certification, stopping, cache identities, and acquisition behavior unchanged.
The current `isar.shadow_model_selection/2` schema authenticates every candidate against the
declared data-likelihood context and records whether shadow evidence was exactly reused, partly
reused, freshly fitted, skipped, or unavailable. Those execution fields are provenance, not inputs
to the standard-selection fingerprint; `/1` shadow records remain readable.
The shadow lane stays AIC-labeled by design (it measures criteria, D6); the authoritative
successor it anticipated landed as evidence selection (H4), with the diagnostics/production
separation carried by the two recorded lanes rather than a promotion ceremony.

**Owner direction (2026-08-20, walkthrough ruling — work tracked as TODO CAL-5): the
degenerate-limit rule.** When one candidate family contains another as a boundary case, the
honest comparison is *free-versus-pinned within the family* — "did the parameter earn its
freedom?" — never an evidence beauty contest between the free model and its own limit. Near the
boundary the two coincide, the extra parameter direction is unconstrained, and the evidence
difference measures the arbitrary prior volume of the unused parameter rather than anything in
the data. A free fit that lands on the boundary resolves to the pinned candidate. First
application: the **resolution lineshape**. Different machines have different resolution shapes
(most Gaussian, some Lorentzian, some genuinely Voigt); the elastic-line fit currently always
fits the full two-width Voigt. The candidate set becomes {Voigt(both widths free),
Gaussian(`lorentz_fwhm`=0), Lorentzian(`gauss_fwhm`=0)}, evidence selects, and the record can
honestly say "we checked the Voigt; the evidence supports the degenerate case" — without paying
for parameters the instrument doesn't use. (Same pattern as §4's delta-function ruling: the
degenerate case is a first-class candidate, not a failure mode or a silent pin.)

**The rule in the fast pass [Implemented in the engine].** Owner direction, 2026-10-23: "for a
DHO convoluted with a resolution, naturally when a peak becomes very low width it becomes
resolution limited; the appropriate response is to model it as a delta function instead of as its
own peak with a width". The fast pass applies the rule to every limit a model can rail at
(`fast_pass._Engine.resolve`, with the shared builder `loop.delta_twin_hypothesis`; the earlier ladder's
`loop._resolve_degenerate` applies it to the same limits until that ladder is retired). At a model's
posterior mode the first screen that hits acts, and its twin is refitted from that point with one
fewer free parameter; except for a railed width, the twin must reach the parent's cost within
`twin_cost_tolerance` (2 deviance units), otherwise the parent stays a model of its own. The
screens, in this order:

| The fit | The model is | Its twin |
|---|---|---|
| a mode's height at or below `twin_height_frac` (1e-3) of the height ceiling | that mode is not there | the count below, same elastic variant, without that mode |
| a mode's energy ω₀ at the zero-energy edge of the window, or its line shape equal to the elastic line's within `elastic_twin_tolerance` | that mode is the elastic line | the count below, with the elastic line |
| the elastic height at or below the same fraction | there is no elastic line | the same count without the elastic line |
| two modes closer than `twin_coincide_fwhm` (0.5) resolution FWHM | one mode | the count below with the pair merged, one pair at a time (exactly coincident delta pairs first); the all-pairs merge is one more candidate |
| a mode's width Γ at the floor | that mode is resolution limited | the same model with that Γ pinned at the floor: a delta-function mode convolved with the resolution |
| the flat background at or below the same fraction of its ceiling | there is no background | the same model without its background |

A mode much narrower than the 0.05 meV convolution grid (Γ below one grid step, blended into point
sampling by two steps; `lineshapes.NARROW_GAMMA_STEPS`) is drawn on the grid by its average against
each node's quadratic B-spline, not by point samples. Point samples alias: a mode at the floor changed
its convolved area by a factor of about 250 as its energy crossed one grid step, which moved its
amplitude and its evidence with it. The B-spline average keeps the area, the centroid and the second
moment independent of where the mode sits between nodes, for both the Stokes side and its Bose-weighted
anti-Stokes partner; ordinary widths are point-sampled as before.

A railed width is the one twin that is not tested against the parent's cost: a width at the floor is
the resolution limit by definition, and the delta mode has its width coordinate and width-prior
factor removed (its covariance row and column are zero), so it is a model of its own with its own
evidence. A width near zero without railing (Γ below 4.5 of its own standard errors, so the data
cannot tell it from the floor) is priced as a delta mask as well. Twins resolve recursively: a twin
that itself rails resolves in turn, and every terminal model keeps exactly one identity
(`ModelResult.model_id`, e.g. `elastic+stokes3+bg_delta2#0`), with each railed ancestor recorded as
`degenerate_twin`, `twin_of` naming that terminal model, probability 0 and no evidence of its own. The
equal prior runs over distinct terminal models only (the 1/(V(k)·2^k) weight of §2), so a railed model
is neither "evidence could not be computed" nor a second vote for its twin, and a clean spectrum's
redundant higher counts do not make it unresolved. A model whose evidence is invalid for any other
reason stays without evidence, with the reason recorded: a Hessian that is not positive definite away
from a bound, or a posterior optimiser that hit `posterior_maxiter`. The energy rule replaces the
half-width energy floor of recipe version 3 (§2.1).

When two genuinely different structures fit equally well, ISAR reports a **fork** — both
answers — rather than averaging them into a single wrong number **[Implemented]**. Columns:
`best_model`, `n_modes`; every mode of the selected count is a row of `report/modes.csv`, ordered
by energy (§2.1, the delivery).

### 2.1 The mode prior recipe, and counting peaks

How many peaks a spectrum holds is a model-selection question, so it is decided the way §2 decides
everything: by evidence, with the Occam penalty paid out of the priors. That penalty is only as
honest as the priors. The **mode prior recipe** (`isar/analyze/prior.py`, version
`isar.mode-prior-recipe/5`) is one rule, set before fitting and identical for every spectrum.
Starting values may come from the data (a seed is not a prior).

**Empirical Bayes, for amplitudes only.** Recipe /4 built every bound from declared quantities and
none from the spectrum's counts: a brighter spectrum then pays no larger Occam penalty for the same
extra peak. Recipe /5 reverses that for the amplitudes, on the operator's ruling (2026-10-25): the
heights are flat on `[0, 1.2 x the spectrum's highest count]`, so the prior is data-derived
(empirical Bayes), and every fit records it (`recipe_provenance`: `empirical_bayes`, `max_count`,
the resulting bounds and the settings). The cost is the usual one for empirical Bayes: the amplitude
prior is set by the data it is then used to explain, so it is a convention for the Occam penalty,
not an independent belief about the amplitude. The margin 1.2 keeps the tallest peak about 6 sigma of headroom at 900 counts.
Widths and energies stay declared.

| Parameter | Prior | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| mode energy E0 (`omega0`) | uniform and ordered on `(1e-3, min(20, highest fitted energy))` meV | the fit window |
| mode width Γ | `1/(Γ + 0.12 meV)` on `(0, 20)` meV, normalised on `[0, 20]` (a softened log-uniform: equal weight per factor of ten, finite at zero, overdamped widths allowed); a delta mode has no width factor | a tenth of the resolution FWHM as the knee; the 20 meV cap |
| mode / elastic height h | flat on `[0, 1.2 x max count]` | the spectrum's highest count |
| flat background (counts per point) | flat on `[0, max count]` | the spectrum's highest count |
| quasi-elastic area | flat on `(0, scale x exposure x resolution FWHM)` (the v4 rule; out of this recipe's scope) | the exposure, the resolution width |

**The height coordinate.** A peak's amplitude coordinate is its convolved height h (glossary): the
maximum of its continuous convolved Stokes-side profile, in counts per point at the spectrum's
reference exposure. The engine's columns are normalised to unit maximum, so h is the linear
coefficient and the flat prior is a genuine prior on h, with no Jacobian. `max count` is the
highest kept count at the reference exposure, `max(y_i / exposure_scale_i)`, floored at one count;
NaN counts are dropped and a spectrum with no finite count is held upstream, never given a default
prior. The width support is `[0, 20]` in the density while the fit's lower bound stays the
`GAMMA_FLOOR` of 1e-3 meV; the density's mass below the floor (about 1.6e-3, 0.002 nats) is not
renormalised. The anti-Stokes partner is still derived from its Stokes mode, never given a prior
(§2, hard rule 2).

**The energy floor, reversed.** Version 3 of the recipe started the energy prior at half a
resolution FWHM to keep a "phonon" from standing in for the elastic line; measured on the endpoint
world, that floor moved the spurious mode up to 0.82 meV rather than removing it. Version 4 removed
the floor (operator direction, 2026-10-23) and recipe /5 keeps it removed. A mode that drifts onto
the elastic line is handled by the degenerate-limit rule (§2): a fit railed at a limit is treated
as its degenerate twin. The window is now capped at 20 meV (E0 is positive, 0 to 20 meV).

**The evidence: posterior mode, exact Hessian, box rule.** The fast pass and `laplace_log_evidence`
take the evidence at the posterior mode (likelihood times prior), with the exact Hessian H of
minus (ln L + ln prior) in the fit coordinates by central differences of the scalar objective
(never J^T J, which is a Gauss-Newton approximation):
`ln Z = ln L + ln prior + (d/2) ln 2 pi - (1/2) ln det H`. The Gaussian must not be priced as if
it did not spread beyond the prior's support, so the integral is taken over the support box. With
C = H^-1 and g the one-sided inward gradient on the coordinates railed at a bound, the quadratic's
centre is mu = theta - C g, and `ln Z = -f(theta) + (1/2) g'Cg + (d/2) ln 2 pi - (1/2) ln det H +
ln P_box`, P_box the mass of N(mu, C) inside the box. It is computed over the coordinates whose
bound lies within 6 sd of mu (the rest contribute 1): one coordinate in closed form (`log_ndtr`),
several by Genz's multivariate normal CDF (relative error about 1e-4, accepted). One railed
coordinate reproduces the single-boundary correction `(g s)^2/2 + ln Phi(-g s)` exactly, so the
older treatment is a special case. A wide coordinate that is not railed is still cut by the box:
f = x^2/5000 on `[-10, 10]` has an unboxed Laplace value of 125.3 against an exact 19.87, and the
box mass 0.1585 puts it right. A Hessian that is not positive definite is `no_evidence` (there is
no linear-limit branch). Laplace is not promised accurate for a prior-dominated coordinate; it is
not priced as if it did not spread beyond the support.

**The sensitivity arms.** `SENSITIVITY_ARMS` names the five alternatives the recipe is checked
against, plus itself: `margin_1.05` and `margin_3` (the amplitude margin), `width_flat` (a flat
width prior), `width_cap_40` (a 40 meV cap) and `today_v4` (recipe /4 in the height coordinate:
heights and background `scale x exposure`, width flat on `[1e-3, 5]`). Changing the margin from 1.05
to 3 costs 1.05 nats per extra peak in the Occam factor (`ln(3/1.05)`), the figure the design
quotes. The check itself, on the real spectra, is run before the beamtime and reported to the
operator **[Planned]**.

**The exposure source.** `exposure_source` (`monitor` or `time`) travels from the normalization
selection into the fit context and still decides the scale of the quasi-elastic area ceiling and of
the `today_v4` arm (`AMPLITUDE_SCALE_PER_MONITOR = 1.0`, `AMPLITUDE_SCALE_PER_SECOND = 1.0e5`,
agent-proposed ceilings from order-of-magnitude instrument figures, no measured value committed). A
context that declares an exposure reference with no source is refused for those, never given a
default scale. The pooled-master path normalises by monitor only: a master with no monitor takes the
nominal exposure `1e6` on the monitor scale, not seconds. That gap is the pooled path's own.

**Mode identities.** The modes of one structure are exchangeable: swapping two labels gives the same
curve, so every solution appears N! times in the likelihood. A flat prior per label then makes the
two evidence lanes disagree by ln N! (6.6 nats at six modes): nested sampling finds all N! copies,
while a Laplace expansion at one fitted point sees one. The recipe orders the energies and puts one
normalised joint density on the ordered region, N!/W^N for N modes in a window of width W. The
Laplace evidence reads it as a function of the set of energies (`ordered_energy_log_prior`) and
nested sampling draws it as the order statistics of uniforms (`ordered_energy_transform`); the
catalogue's prior bundle carries both (`logpdf_ordered`, `transform_ordered`). Each lane gets the
N! exactly once and the two agree. The ordering is on E0 (`omega0`), the sampled coordinate, where that
normalisation is exact. It applies only to exchangeable
modes: every other parameter of each mode (Γ, h) must carry the same prior density as the others. The
modes are split into groups that are exchangeable among themselves, and each group of two or more is
ordered on its own, N!/W^N per group. A prior that singles out one mode's amplitude or width puts
that mode in a group of its own, so its energy keeps the labelled prior. The recipe's own width
prior is not part of that comparison: a delta-function mode (Γ pinned at the floor by a degenerate
twin, §2, and so with no width factor) is ordered together with the finite-width modes, one k!, one
ordered transform over every mode's E0.

**The k! in the Laplace path.** The Laplace expansion at one labelled mode with `ln(k!/W^k)` equals
the integral over all k! label copies with the flat `1/W^k`. Omitting the k! would add a spurious
ln(k+1) penalty per extra peak. It is checked (`tests/analyze/test_prior_v5.py`) on two peaks of
equal height with free positions: the brute-force grid gives ln Z = -237.3786, Laplace with the k!
gives -237.3789, and without it -238.0720. Two peaks of fixed, different heights with only positions
free have one label copy, so a k! test must use equal heights or free all of each peak's
parameters, or it cannot tell right from wrong.

**Status [Implemented; sensitivity check in S1U7].** The recipe, the ordered prior, the box-rule
Laplace, the softened log-uniform width and the signature are implemented and tested. The fitter's
bounds come from the recipe (`loop.build_problem` reads `prior.mode_bounds`, which resolves
`prior.resolve_prior_for`, so the TRF box and the Laplace box are one source), as do rung 1 of the
count ladder and both evidence lanes (`evidence.py`, `errors/bayes.py`), which price modes under the
ordered energy prior. The campaign path's `PriorBundle` carries the same width density
(`ResolvedPrior.kind = "softlog"`). The delivery (`scan_delivery.fit_one`) passes the card's
`max_modes` to the fit and runs rung 2 (the earlier count ladder, which the fast pass replaces; §2.2). Not yet run: the sensitivity check on the real spectra.

**The delivery [Implemented].** Every spectrum is in one state (`isar.analyze.count_ladder`):
`settled`, `provisional` (rung 2 still to run), `search limited`, `unresolved` or `held`, with its
reasons, the rung that decided it, the probability of each count the deciding rung priced and the
ambiguity marker, recorded as `fit_selection_semantics["count_ladder"]` and projected onto `results.csv`
(`count_state`, `count_state_reason`, `deciding_rung`, `count_probability`, `runner_up_count`,
`runner_up_probability`, `count_ambiguous`, `count_flags`; the selected count is `n_modes`). The record
lists every count rung 1 tried, each with its evidence (rung 2's nested lnZ, else rung 1's Laplace lnZ)
or the reason it has none: `unconverged`, `resolved to <twin>`, `not sampled at rung 2`, or
`sampling failed: <reason>`. A scan with no fit reads `held` (with its hold reason), `fitting`

the ladder existed reads `not recorded`. Each
mode of the selected count is a row of `report/modes.csv`: intrinsic ω₀ and the displayed peak

dispersion prior (`evidence_update._master_prior_admissible`). A pooled master runs rung 2 to
completion inside its own fit.

**The mode ceiling.** A spectrum may hold up to a configurable number of modes: card

### 2.2 The fast pass: a seed, then the full search **[Partial: engine built, synthetic count aims met; wall time above its aim; four thresholds provisional]**

How many modes a spectrum holds, where they are and how sure the answer is: one search that aims at about
a minute per spectrum on a laptop (an aim and a diagnostic, never a stopping rule). The code is
`isar/analyze/fast_pass.py` (`run_fast_pass`); the settings are `FastPassSettings`. It runs no nested
sampling, profile interval, bootstrap or automatic second pass. It replaces the earlier count ladder
(a Laplace rung and a nested-sampling rung with seven triggers), which stays in the code until
`scan_delivery.fit_one` calls the fast pass; §2.1's "delivery" paragraph still describes that pipeline.

**Stage 1, the seed.** Pseudo-Voigt peaks, each with its anti-Stokes partner tied by the Bose factor and no
convolution, so it is cheap. Fitted on the original counts (never on subtracted residuals): candidate
positions are ranked by how much they improve the Poisson fit, all seed peaks are refitted jointly after each
insertion so a centre can move, and the search stops when no candidate adds `seed_stop_gain` (a provisional
2·Δln L) or at the mode ceiling. The seed's only outputs are starting points. It never limits stage 2.

**Stage 2, the full search.** Every count from 0 to the ceiling is fitted, with and without the elastic line
and the background where admissible, every parameter free, from several starts: the seed, the previous count's
fit plus a residual feature, splits of every fitted peak, the card's declared peaks and perturbed restarts.
Nothing is skipped because the seed did not suggest it. The counts that contend are then audited: a split of
every fitted peak in both orientations (heavy-low and heavy-high) at narrow and resolution-scale separations,
shoulder starts, moved-centre and changed-width starts, a broad component added across the window even when the
residuals look clean, and deletions and merges downwards (`audit_rounds`). Each fit is bounded least squares
(the trust-region reflective method) on the exact signed Poisson-deviance residuals. Degenerate and delta-mode
twins resolve as in §2, every distinct solution is priced by the box-rule Laplace evidence at its posterior
mode, and the counts are weighed by the 1/(V(k)·2^k) model prior. A count is *contending* when its evidence
lies within `contending_margin` (10 nats) of the best; a count with no priced basin at all contends when its
best log-likelihood reaches the selected model's (a count with any priced basin has evidence, whatever its
degenerate basins do). One that fits worse and cannot be priced is recorded and does not block the result.

**The mode ceiling is 6, configurable (card `[fitting] max_modes`), and arbitrary.** It is a known limit set by
the operator, not a measured one ("6 is arbitrary"): a best count at the ceiling reads *search limited
(ceiling)*, and the result says so rather than claiming a count.

**The four states.**

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `resolved` | the preferred count carries at least `close_call_p` (0.80) of the probability, every contending count has evidence |
| `close_call` | the alternatives together carry 0.20 or more; the material alternatives are listed (`close_call_alternatives`), the shown fit is the most probable count, and `numerically_marginal` says when the evidence's own numerical spread could move a count across the 0.80 line |
| `unresolved` | a contending count's evidence could not be computed (singular or boundary-limited curvature, or the posterior optimiser hit its iteration limit): no confident winner, the probabilities are conditional on the counts that have evidence, and the spectrum is a deep-pass candidate. A failed calculation is never evidence against a count |
| `search_limited` | the mode ceiling or the per-spectrum safety budget (`safety_budget_s`, 300 s single-core) stopped the search; `search_limit_reason` names which |

When more than one applies the order is unresolved, search limited, close call, resolved.
`probabilities_conditional` is set when the state is `unresolved`; `deep_pass_candidate` is set for every state
but `resolved`.

**Confident and tentative peaks.** The result's peaks are the selected model's. A peak is *confident* when all
three hold: removing it with everything else held fixed changes the predicted counts by at least the anchor on
the visibility scale (`deletion_impact`, `confident_anchor`, which may depend on the spectrum's highest count);
it has a one-to-one match within one resolution FWHM in every credible alternative (probability at least
`material_p`, or a contender with no evidence); and no flag interval lies within one FWHM of it. An uncertain extra
peak therefore does not demote stable ones. Every other peak is *tentative*: it keeps its parameters in the
record and feeds the close call and the flags, but is not reported as found.

**Regime and status, kept apart.** A peak's physical *regime* is `near_elastic` (within two resolution FWHM of
zero), `overdamped` (Γ ≥ ω₀, or |corr(ω₀, Γ)| above `overdamped_correlation`) or `underdamped`; the first two
are marked `manual_fit`. Each parameter's *status* (`constrained`, `model_dependent`, `boundary_limited`,
`undetermined`) is a separate statement about the covariance: a singular covariance makes it `undetermined`, a
delta mode's width is `boundary_limited`. The regime criteria are warning heuristics, not proof that an
energy is unmeasurable.

**Areas.** Each peak's Stokes and both-side areas come from its height, ω₀ and Γ, with an uncertainty by the
delta method through the full covariance (so the anticorrelation of neighbouring heights is kept). `area_status`
is `usable`, `decomposition_conditional` (a correlated neighbour could trade area with it) or `unavailable`. Where
only a sum is constrained, `grouped_intensity` gives it over the stated energy interval.

**The flags.** A within-spectrum flag, "possible hidden component near X meV; a neighbour may be absorbing it",
carries one of two reasons. *Structured residual*: a signed-deviance scan with one-FWHM windows finds a
discrepancy at or above `flag_residual_threshold`. *Ambiguous decomposition*: a correlated height pair, or a
material broad or shoulder alternative with a component more than one FWHM from every selected peak (its
statistic is the evidence gap in nats). The second kind says the assignment of intensity is ambiguous, not that
a hidden component exists.

**Reclassify.** `flag_statistics` holds every raw statistic behind a threshold, so `reclassify(result,
settings)` and `reclassify_record(record, settings)` re-apply `confident_anchor`, `flag_residual_threshold`,
`overdamped_correlation`, `close_call_p` and `material_p` with no refit. Changing a search setting raises
`ValueError` naming it.

**Timing.** `result.timing` carries seconds per stage (`seed`, `ladder`, `audits`, `twins`, `evidence`,
`classify`, `total`), fits and seconds per start family, the seed's peak count, and `n_fits`.

**Limits, stated where they bite.**

- *Four thresholds are provisional.* `seed_stop_gain`, `confident_anchor`, `flag_residual_threshold` (0 means
  that flag kind is off) and `overdamped_correlation` are agent defaults, to be calibrated on synthetic spectra
  before the fast pass counts as Implemented. The close-call and contending margins are rulings and existing
  constants.
- *The probabilities are model-dependent.* They are empirical-Bayes under the priors of §2.1, and the 2^k delta
  mask weight is a modelling choice: it moves a clean spectrum's count probabilities by a few nats at most.
  They come from Laplace evidence; whether they track nested-sampling evidence is what the deep pass checks.
- *Laplace leans toward fewer modes.* On the earlier ladder's tuning stream, the Laplace value of the count above
  the best sat 15 to 18 nats below its nested-sampling value in three of four priced cases (measured 2026-10-23
  with 400 live points; the measurement script is scratch, not committed). The count decisions checked there did
  not flip at 400 points. The fast pass is Laplace-only, so the bias applies to it; the deep pass is what checks it.
- *128 live points are not enough at 11 to 14 free parameters.* On single contenders with 11 and 14 free
  parameters, nested-sampling lnZ at 128 points lay 31 to 54 nats from its 400-point value, while reporting
  ±1 to 1.5 nats. `logzerr` does not bound that bias; a sampled reference needs far more live points than
  the earlier lane used.
- *A confident list is a screen, not a detector.* False positives and negatives on weak peaks are ordinary.
- *The ceiling is arbitrary* (above).

**Acceptance (synthetic).** A clean three-peak spectrum (ω₀ 5, 9 and 14 meV; an elastic line; a background; a
Voigt resolution of 1.5 meV FWHM), ten Poisson draws, plus the same truth with a fourth, planted narrow peak
at 17.5 meV (Γ 0.05 and 0) sized to a noiseless deletion grade of 100, run at the production defaults:
`set ISAR_FAST_PASS_ACCEPTANCE=1` then `python -m pytest tests/analyze/test_fast_pass_acceptance.py -m slow
-s -p no:xdist` (a table of twelve rows in its own process pool; slow-marked and opt-in by that variable, run
on purpose, never by a routine check; it prints the aims as reached and asserts none). A draw passes when each true peak is
matched one-to-one by a confident peak within 0.75 meV, no confident peak is left over, and the state is
`resolved` at three or `close_call` with P(3) ≥ 0.05. The aim is 9 of 10 draws passing, none unresolved, and
both narrow peaks found as a confident peak within one FWHM with ln Z(4) − ln Z(3) > 10. **Measurements, 2026-10-26,
production defaults, six parallel processes.** The first table missed (1 of 10 draws, 9 of 12 rows
unresolved): a high count's best-fitting basin (an extra mode railed against its bound, a Hessian not positive
definite) had no evidence and, although the same count had priced basins, made it contend. A count now
contends unpriced only when none of its basins is priced (the prototype's rule), and the one rerun gives 9 of
10 draws passing (the miss, seed 11, is resolved at four with the extra mode at 18.4 meV only tentative), no
row unresolved, and both narrow peaks passing; in every draw the three confident peaks are the three true
peaks (within 0.1 meV). With the delta-twin rule off the same table has no passing draw and all twelve rows
unresolved. Wall time per spectrum has a median of 84 s and a maximum of 215 s against the 60 s aim (six
processes sharing the machine), still above it. The table is reported, not a gate, and is not rerun to chase
the aim.

### 2.3 The peak-count benchmark **[Built; targets not yet measured at the design's draw count]**

`isar/synth/count_bench.py` tests the ladder on truths the fitter never sees, against the challenge: fit any
spectrum a human could fit. The generator draws synthetic spectra with 0 to the ceiling modes (default 6):
energies, widths, amplitudes, temperature, elastic strength, background, exposure and a Voigt resolution.
Each mode's anti-Stokes partner is the production Bose tie, and counts are Poisson with no floor. Hard kinds
are over-represented: close doublets, broad single peaks, a weak peak on a strong neighbour's tail, features
near the elastic line, empty spectra and generic spectra of 2 to the ceiling modes. Each case is fitted
through `scan_delivery.fit_one`, the production path, from an observation that carries no truth.

**What "resolvable" means.** A truth with N modes is *resolvable* when two conditions hold over repeated
Poisson draws of it. A reference fit prefers N modes over N−1 in at least 90% of the draws of the truth. And
the same comparison on the truth with its weakest mode removed prefers N in at most 5% of its draws. The
design's default is 200 draws. A truth that is not resolvable is *borderline*, and on a borderline case the
fitter is not required to recover the count. The reference is the production Laplace evidence (default
`--ref-evidence laplace`), with truth-placed starts and eight random starts per model. `--ref-evidence nested`
is the sampler route: about three hours per resolvable two-mode case at the design's draw count. The reference
shares rung 1's method and so its blind spot: the limit on Laplace in §2.2 applies to these labels too. Its
agreement with the truth is reported beside the figures.

**The figures.** All three are targets (rulings R5, R14, R15), reported with numerator, denominator, binomial
standard error and a Wilson 95% interval. None is a gate.

1. *Correct counts on resolvable cases:* target at least 90%. An ambiguous answer on a resolvable case is an
   abstention: it is not correct, and it stays in the denominator.
2. *Confident extra modes on empty or single-mode truths:* target at most 5%. A confident extra mode is a count
   above the truth's that the fit does not mark ambiguous.
3. *The A/B test:* for each resolvable case, 1000 fresh draws from the truth and 1000 from the fitted curve. The
   likelihood-ratio test picks the source of each draw. A case passes when its accuracy is at most 60%; 50% is a
   coin flip. The figure is the share of resolvable cases that pass. No share target is set.

A figure that stalls is reported as reached, not tuned overnight.

**Status, 2026-10-23.**

- The design's draw count has not been run. A probe measured 1.6 to 2.1 s per draw pair of the Laplace reference
  (one worker), so one resolvable two-mode case at 200 plus 200 draws takes about 12 minutes; nested sampling would
  take hours. The full characterisation therefore runs only on request.
- The tuning run used 20 draws (cases T0000 to T0059 at ceiling 4; 57 finished). It labelled 1 of 57 truths
  resolvable, and many clean single peaks came out borderline. At 10 null draws the 5% rule allows no false split at
  all. None of the three figures was measured meaningfully.
- The same run, with rung 2 forced on every contending set, moved the count on 10 of 56 spectra: 4 towards the truth
  and 4 away. Correct counts were 37 of 56 at both rungs. This is a check on synthetic cases that set the triggers'
  starting thresholds, not a figure against the targets.
- On one resolvable smoke case with a correct count, the A/B test picked the source 93.5 to 95% of the time, far
  above the 60% target. With thousands of counts a fit differs detectably from its truth, so the A/B target is
  expected to be hard on well-counted cases.

**Running it.**

- `python -m isar.synth.count_bench --mode tuning`: the capped tuning run (at most 60 cases and 30 minutes, on the
  tuning seeds).
- `run-isar-count-bench.bat --cases 4 --draws 3 --null-draws 3 --ab-draws 200`: a quick look. Output goes to
  `out\count_bench\<timestamp>\` (`figures.json`, `cases.csv`, `count_bench.log`), and the folder and wall-clock
  time are printed.
- The full run (`--cases 100 --draws 200`, the defaults) takes hours. It runs only when started.
- The routine suite runs only the fast subset (`-m "not slow"`).

## 3. Certify: the interval ladder, with its coverage measured

The fast error bar every tool quotes is the Hessian ("HESSE"): invert the curvature of the cost
at the minimum. It assumes the cost is a clean parabola. ISAR computes it
(`hesse_lo`/`hesse_hi`) **[Implemented]**, then climbs: a profile-likelihood (MINOS) interval,
which can be asymmetric (`omega0_profile_lo/hi`) **[Implemented]**; a parametric bootstrap —
M refits of Poisson redraws — as the ordinary physical-fit authoritative interval
(`interval_lo/hi`) **[Implemented]**; and a Bayesian credible interval as the top single-fit rung
**[Implemented, diagnostics-only]**. Pooled-master certification uses the separate mixed interval
described in §4.1.

A ladder is only worth its cost if the rungs differ measurably, so we measured them. The claim
"a 68% interval covers the truth 68% of the time" can only be tested where the truth is known,
which is why the test is synthetic by construction (this is the calibration tier of ISAR's
validation; the real-data tier tests reasonableness against a human, not calibration). What was
controlled:

- the generator is *identical* to the fitted model — deliberately. In inverse-problems terms
  this is the **inverse crime**, and it is committed on purpose: sharing the model isolates the
  optimizer and the error machinery from any model-mismatch, so a failure can only be the
  fitter's fault. The crime is then broken deliberately in the robustness tier — and the two
  stages are kept distinct, because a clean inverse-crime pass must never be mistaken for "the
  fitter works on real data" (misspecification is Chapter 6's subject);
- the truth is exact and per-scan; coverage is tallied directly against it;
- all three methods are evaluated on the **same** per-scan fits — a paired comparison, not
  three separate ensembles;
- every scan is deterministically seeded and worker-count-invariant;
- N = 200 scans per cell, so the binomial standard error at p = 0.68 is ±0.033 — wide enough
  to be honest about, quoted on every cell.

### 3.1 The honest headline: on ω₀, the cheap error bar holds

We expected HESSE to under-cover and the bootstrap to rescue it. That is **not** what the
experiment shows, and we report what it shows. On ω₀ the three rungs are statistically
indistinguishable at every count level — 0.68/0.68/0.64 at 15 counts, 0.66/0.66/0.63 at 30,
0.60/0.60/0.61 at 100 (Hessian/profile/bootstrap, ±0.03 each). ω₀ is a location parameter with
a nearly parabolic likelihood; the Bernstein–von Mises regime arrives early, and the HESSE
number is genuinely fine *when the fit is healthy*. (The same held in ISAR's Phase-3 method
comparison; this is a consistent result, not a one-off.)

Two things in Fig. 5 still demand explanation, and neither flatters the machinery:

**All rungs dip together at high counts.** At 100 expected peak counts every method covers at
~0.60–0.61, about 2 SE below nominal, with zero central-value bias (mean pull −0.03, bias
< 0.001 meV). The pull width tells the story: 1.01 at 15 counts → 1.09 at 100. There is a small,
count-independent scatter floor in the derived ω₀ (~0.01 meV, from the initial-condition grid,
optimizer tolerance, and the ω₀ = √(Ω_d² + Γ²) reparameterization the fit used when these figures were measured) that no per-fit interval —
curvature, profile, or bootstrap — can see, because all three are built on the same fit. It is
negligible when σ ≈ 0.09 meV and eats ~7% of coverage when the interval tightens to
σ ≈ 0.034 meV. This is a preview of Chapter 6: an interval can only account for the
uncertainty its model contains.

**Coverage is a property of an ensemble you will never have.** The table says "68% of *200
scans*". You get *one*. An error bar that is right on average and wrong scan-to-scan is
indistinguishable, on your one scan, from an error bar that is simply right — unless you can
check it *on that scan*. That is the actual argument for the ladder, and it is Fig. 6.

### 3.2 The single-scan argument: agreement is checkable, coverage is not

Fig. 6 plots, for every scan in the ensemble, the ratio of its HESSE width to its bootstrap
width. On healthy ω₀ fits the two agree within ±25% for ~96% of scans — which is *why*
`hesse_trusted` can certify the fast tier. But push Γ toward the resolution floor
(Γ_true = 0.1 meV at 15 counts, where the bootstrap Γ interval is visibly right-skewed, median
asymmetry 1.31) and the per-scan agreement disintegrates: the median HESSE/bootstrap width
ratio rises to 1.18 and **51% of scans fall outside the agreement band** — some HESSE bars
half the bootstrap width, some double. The *ensemble* Γ coverage in that same cell still looks
respectable (0.77 vs 0.86 excluding at-bound fits) — the average hides exactly what the
experimenter needs to know about their one scan. Including the at-bound fits, HESSE Γ coverage
drops to 0.64 while the bootstrap holds 0.81: the failure concentrates in the scans the wall
chapter is about.

A concrete mis-certification, from the 2026-07-10 deterministic re-certification (log in git
history): at q=0.45 a converged scan with `interval_reliable=False` but `hesse_trusted=True`
certified the HESSE interval [3.8248, 3.8276] against a truth of 3.8967 — outside by
0.070 meV, roughly **35 half-widths**. Its γ = 0.0134 passed the at-bound heuristic, so the
trust gate (covariance_ok ∧ ¬gamma_at_bound ∧ counts) admitted it. The lesson is standing: a
covariance-based trust gate needs the same calibration scrutiny as the bootstrap flag — a γ
near but not *at* the bound can still fool it.

Γ-at-bound test to each mode's own Γ, so a rival mode railed at the floor no longer distrusts a
well-fitted target beside it (`scan_delivery._mode_hesse_trusted`; the fit-level flag remains the
primary mode's).

## 4. When the ladder breaks: the Γ wall

Every rung in Chapter 3 assumed the fit found an *interior* minimum. On a resolution-limited
spike it does not: the fitted damping Γ runs into its lower bound and stays there. At the bound
there is no parabola to invert — the HESSE σ_Γ is undefined-in-principle, whatever number the
matrix inversion returns — and the parametric bootstrap collapses, because every redraw refits
to the same wall (Fig. 7a).

Fig. 7 shows both outcomes on real archived data, re-fit standalone (M = 200 bootstrap refits,
seeded) from the workspace's archived calibration. Scan 529 is healthy: an interior Γ minimum,
a bootstrap ω₀ distribution with believable spread, 68% interval [5.103, 5.125],
`interval_reliable=True`. Scan 518 is the pathology: the distribution collapses to
[3.1478, 3.1500] — width 0.0022 meV. A naive reader would quote σ(ω₀) ≈ 0.001 meV, three
orders of magnitude tighter than the ~1 meV instrument resolution. Zero width is degeneracy,
not precision.

- optimizer failure — the fit converged (`cost_per_dof` ≈ 2.1: imperfect, as expected for a

provisional band is honest where a 0.001 meV bar would be a lie. (Reporting the collapsed

### 4.1 Campaign certification is a pooled, model-robust statement

A scan verdict and a campaign decision answer different questions. Individual fits are useful
diagnostics, but repeated measurements at the same `(q,T)` are one combine-before-divide Poisson
observation. Only that pooled master can certify ω or update the authoritative dispersion prior.

Before certification, ISAR checks that exposure-scaled member spectra are homogeneous with a seeded
parametric-bootstrap Poisson deviance test (default α=0.01). The test masks rather than removes bad
points and treats zero counts exactly. A failure says “this pool is internally inconsistent”; it does
not license automatic exclusion or prove the requested physics infeasible.

The certifying ω interval is taken across the retained model-weighted full-parameter predictive
ensemble, not from whichever individual scan or single fitted structure happened to look best. In
the current `isar.master_predictive/2` contract, the parameter particles are selected model by model:
nested posterior resamples first, then parametric-bootstrap draws, then a trusted HESSE/Laplace
approximation. Retained model weights (evidence posteriors since H4; the record names its
`model_weight_kind`) are fixed at their observed-data values. The resulting
`model_interval` is therefore a conditional-on-target-present, heterogeneous mixed-particle quantile;
it is neither a Bayesian model-averaged credible interval nor a bootstrap distribution of the complete
refit-and-reselect procedure. No-target mass is not included in its ω quantiles.

That interval retains active **legacy** master-certification and stopping authority so STAT-1 changes
no behavior. Its semantics explicitly make it ineligible for EIG and acquisition. The distinction
still resolves the Γ wall operationally: Γ can sit at the instrumental resolution boundary while ω
remains identifiable after mixing over retained parameter and model uncertainty. Conversely,
unresolved ω multimodality, label switching, target-track ambiguity, or pool inconsistency blocks
certification no matter how narrow one model's HESSE bar looks. Coverage and unconditional
false-certification calibration of this actual mixed delivery remain required before changing its
scientific authority.

The final-certification computation preserves that statistical unit. It refits—or exactly reuses—one
frozen pooled master per requested `(q,T)`, never the physical scans that contributed its counts.
Completed master units checkpoint immediately, but the authoritative generation is published only
when the entire objective-path worklist is complete. Thus interruption changes elapsed time, not the
sample, seed, model inputs, reduction order, or reported interval.

## 5. Bayesian and probability-like pieces

Chapter 0 separates three related outputs: normalized branch-assignment weights, a
prior-regularized MAP point estimate, and diagnostics-only nested-sampler credible intervals. A MAP
estimate is not by itself a stored posterior distribution or interval.

**The branch-assignment score distribution [Implemented].** ISAR reports `branch_id`, normalized
assignment weights in `branch_posterior`, and their Shannon entropy `assignment_entropy`. The
versioned scorer combines likelihood-like compatibility with explicit assignment rules and
penalties. The weights are useful for ranking and thresholding, but no reliability campaign or
fully normalized generative model currently earns calibrated-posterior language. Fig. 8 contrasts
scan 505 (entropy 0.008 — concentrated, weight 0.999
acoustic) with scan 477 (entropy 0.646 of a possible ln 2 ≈ 0.693 — a coin flip, 0.653 vs
0.347). The entropy *number* is the deliverable: near 0, the assignment can be banked into the
dispersion prior; near ln 2, ISAR reports the ambiguity instead of guessing a branch and
poisoning the prior with it. The figure renders the archived campaign's numbers — provenance,
not re-derivation.

![FIG. 8](stats/figures/branch_posterior.png)

**FIG. 8.** The normalized branch-assignment weights for a concentrated and an ambiguous scan of
`mvp_zone_test16`; entropy records concentration over the declared assignment state space.

**The dispersion-prior MAP point estimate [Implemented].** When the campaign has a belief about
ω₀ at a q-point, the fit can be softly constrained by it; `prior_omega`, `prior_sigma`, `prior_n`
record the regularizing prior and `fit_kind` records that it was used. The optimizer returns a MAP
point estimate, not a stored posterior distribution or credible interval. The data-only cost is
kept separate (`cost` vs `posterior_cost`), but the data cost is evaluated at the constrained
optimum. STAT-1 therefore labels effective soft constraints and unapproved hard conditioning as
selection-ineligible while retaining the legacy scores. STAT-2a now observes comparable
unconstrained selection fits beside constrained delivery fits without changing authority. Under
H4 the separation is enforced in production: evidence selection consumes transported
(prespecified-origin) priors exactly once, and `data_dependent_rescue` / `post_selection`
conditioning remains selection-ineligible.

**Nested-sampler credible intervals [Implemented, diagnostics-only].** Under `--errors bayes` the
nested sampler runs on the preliminary-best label and records `bayes_interval`,
`bayes_gamma_interval`, posterior samples, and its own
convergence gates (`bayes_ok`, `bayes_ess`, `bayes_fail_reason`). The frequentist↔Bayesian
agreement check is live as a per-scan trust signal of the §3.2 kind (`bayes_freq_ratio`).

These samples are a Bayesian posterior under an optimizer-bound, data-dependent prior kernel.
That makes the individual posterior interval a coherent conditional result under that kernel, but
its `log_evidence` is not comparable across models or campaigns, and the current system is not a
Bayesian model average. What remains **[Planned]** is the *licence*, and it splits into two different kinds of waiting:
the single-fit interval's promotion to a certifying rung needs the **coverage campaign** (TODO
CAL-2) — the scoring machinery for it exists, so that gate is owner go-ahead plus compute, with
one technical settle inside it (the `nlive` 250-vs-400 discrepancy on very-high-count scans);
whereas any formal Bayesian model-average or evidence-comparison authority needs **real design
and implementation work** (plans/stats-audit.md STAT-5): a scientific prior registry replacing
the data-dependent box kernel, prior normalization and sensitivity, all-model coverage including
the null, evidence-uncertainty propagation, SBC/PPC, and calibration. A physical row's `bayes_interval` remains
diagnostic; §4.1 separately describes the legacy mixed master interval.

## 6. The honest caveat: these intervals are statistical-only

This is the most important thing to know before you use an ISAR uncertainty.

ISAR's bootstrap interval captures **counting statistics only**. It does **not** include
systematic uncertainty — the background shape, the resolution function, energy-zero drift
between scans. Chapter 3 sharpens this rather than softening it: *within* its own statistical
universe the machinery is calibrated (Fig. 4), the high-count dip is itself a measured example
of uncertainty the per-fit interval cannot contain, and that is exactly why the 18%
gold-set coverage against a human's values is diagnostic of missing *systematics*, not of
broken statistics. On well-counted data the counting error is tiny, so the interval is tight;
the human's value falls inside ISAR's 68% interval far less than 68% of the time. **That is
expected from a statistical-only interval, and it means the error bars are too small to use as
final uncertainties.**

What *is* reliable is the **central value**. On the gold set ISAR's energy lands a median of
~0.16 meV from the human's — well inside the ~1 meV instrument resolution. So:

> **Practical rule:** treat ISAR's energy as a good ballpark and its confidence as triage
> guidance. Do **not** weight by ISAR's σ or quote it as a final uncertainty — measure that
> yourself once you've looked at the scan. A systematics-aware error model is future work.

This is why the reports separate **parameter recovery** (good — the number is in the right
place) from **interval calibration** (a known limitation — the *width* is statistical-only),
and why the validation runs in two tiers: synthetic truth tests calibration, real-data
comparison tests reasonableness and safety against a provisional human reference. Agreeing
*exactly* with the human was never the goal.

### 6.1 What "coverage" means here, and the 2026-09-03 campaign that missed it

Coverage is a frequency, not a property of one number. A published 68% interval claims that if you
repeated the measurement many times, the truth would fall inside the interval about 68% of the
time. You check it by measuring many things whose truth you already know and counting.

The live targeted campaign of 2026-09-03 — 82 scans along (2,0,0)→(3,0,0), scored against the
analytic `phonon_dft` truth — gave **0.444 coverage against a nominal 0.68** on a 0.05 meV bar. The
truth landed outside the published interval on more than half the points; the intervals are too
narrow by roughly **1.9×**. The central values were fine (median |Δω₀| = 0.028 meV): this is a
width-and-centre problem, not a "the fitter found the wrong peak" problem.

The scorer splits that miss two ways, and the split is what decides the fix:

- **Random scatter** — the interval is honestly centred but too narrow. This is what an inflation
  factor is for: multiply every published σ by a calibrated *k* and coverage climbs to nominal.
- **A systematic centre pull** — every fitted centre is displaced in the same direction (mean Δω₀
  was −0.027 meV). **No inflation can fix a pull.** Widening an interval that sits in the wrong
  place does buy coverage, but only by being wrong more loosely; the bias is still there and it
  does not average away over more scans.

Roughly half the 2026-09-03 miss was pull. Part of it was ISAR's own doing — the DHO convolution
grid quantised fitted centres — and refitting the same 82 scans on a finer grid halved both the
error and the pull (0.021 → 0.012 meV median, −0.027 → −0.013 mean). That grid is now 0.01 meV
(`CONVOLUTION_DX` in `isar/analyze/lineshapes.py`).

What remains after the grid fix is suspected to be **resolution focusing**: on a triple-axis
instrument the resolution ellipsoid is tilted in (Q, ω), so a dispersive branch is sampled
asymmetrically across the peak and the measured centroid is pulled along the dispersion. ISAR
convolves with a 1D energy-resolution Gaussian and therefore cannot see this at all. The proper fix
is to deconvolve with the full 4D resolution function (or fit in the resolution's own frame); it is
**deliberately deferred**, and until it lands the honest response is a wider default bar — ±0.1 meV
— rather than a calibration constant that would launder a bias into a bigger error bar.

## 8. The equations, in one place

The podium sheet (owner request, 2026-08-20 walkthrough): every statistical form the preceding
chapters explain, stated once. Tags: **[code]** = transcribed from the implementation named
beside it (authoritative for parameterization); **[def]** = the defining form the machinery
realizes. `y` = counts, `f` = model expected counts.

**Counting and cost (§1)**

- Poisson: `P(n|mu) = mu^n e^(-mu) / n!`; stored variance `sigma = sqrt(N)`, with `sigma(0) = 0`
  — no floor (convention D3). **[def]**
- Baker–Cousins (the minimized cost) **[code — `analyze/estimators.py`]**:
  `C = 2 * sum_i [ f_i - y_i + y_i ln(y_i/f_i) ]`, the `y=0` term exactly `f_i`.
  Convention `errordef = 1`: the 1σ contour is `ΔC = 1`; `cost_per_dof = C / dof` reads like a
  reduced χ².
- The straddle (§1's identity) **[code]**: Neyman `sum (y-f)^2 / max(y,1)` minimizes (constant
  rate) to the **harmonic mean**; modified Pearson `sum (y-f)^2 / f` to the **RMS**;
  Baker–Cousins to the **arithmetic mean** — the two χ²'s provably bracket the likelihood
  answer.

**Lineshapes and physics gates (§2, ARCHITECTURE §7)**

- DHO, full form (paper Eq. 2) **[code — `analyze/lineshapes.py` `dho_signal`; `analyze/components.py` `PhononDHO`]**:
  `S(E) = A (E0/π) 4Γ y(E) / [ (E² − E0²)² + 4Γ²E² ]`, with `y(E) = (n(E)+1)·E = E / (−expm1(−E/kT))`
  evaluated at every energy point, so `y(0) = kT`. `E0` is the undamped energy (`omega0`), `Γ` the
  damping (HWHM), `A` the intrinsic narrow-limit amplitude. The Stokes side is `E ≥ 0` and the
  anti-Stokes side `E < 0` comes from the same formula, so detailed balance holds pointwise. The
  narrow-limit Stokes area is `A(n(E0)+1)`, the anti-Stokes area `A·n(E0)`. There is no underdamping
  constraint: an overdamped mode (`Γ > E0`) is an ordinary point of the model.
- Height coordinate **[code — `analyze/components.py` `PhononDHO`, `lineshapes.UnitPeak`]**: the
  fitted amplitude is the convolved height `h`, the maximum over `E ≥ 0` of the whole convolved
  profile (both sides, so the anti-Stokes tail counts), in counts at the spectrum's exposure. The
  model mean is `exposure × h × (profile / height)`, and the derived intrinsic amplitude is
  `A = h / height`.
- Narrow poles **[code — `lineshapes._unit_dho`]**: point samples of the formula alias on the
  convolution grid (a Γ = 1e-3 meV mode's area moved by a factor 250 as it crossed one 0.05 meV
  step), so each pole narrower than two grid steps adds an area-preserving correction: its point
  samples are replaced by their average against each node's quadratic B-spline. The Bose numerator
  is taken at the complex pole, which removes the dip of width Γ that a node under the pole would
  otherwise show. A smoothstep between one and two grid steps is the only blend; below one step the
  same rendering runs down to Γ = 1e-9 meV, so the column is smooth through Γ = 0 and the optimiser
  meets no branch switch.
- Evaluation cost, measured 2026-10-25 (diagnostic, not a requirement): `CompositeModel.evaluate` on
  the a06 curve (145 points, T = 300 K, elastic and background present, shared machine) took
  0.048 / 0.070 / 0.103 ms for 1 / 3 / 6 modes before the full DHO and 0.094 / 0.209 / 0.365 ms
  after, about 2x at one mode and 3.5x at six: one height search and one pole correction per mode.
- Validation **[test — `tests/analyze/test_dho_full.py`]**: as Γ → 0 at fixed E0 the convolved model
  approaches the two resolution curves at ±E0 with their Bose weights; it is continuous through
  critical damping (Γ = E0) and finite as E0 → 0; grid refinement and a wider support change nothing;
  the column is smooth through Γ = 0. Not promised: exact areas at the corner of small E0 with large Γ
  (E0 near 1e-3 meV, Γ near 20 meV), where the grid's support limits the tail, and the kernel table's
  own truncation.
- Detailed balance (Hard Rule 2) **[def]**: `I_AS / I_S = exp(-hbar w / k_B T)` — the
  anti-Stokes amplitude is *derived*, never fit; admissibility of an anti-Stokes candidate is
  gated on this before any fit. The thermal factor sits inside the lineshape, so amplitude
  transport is physical (ruling H4(a); `analyze/components.py`).
- Resolution **[code — `analyze/lineshapes.py`]**: Gaussian and Lorentzian FWHM-parameterized;
  Voigt via the Faddeeva function `V(x) = A * Re[w(z)] / (sigma sqrt(2 pi))` with
  `z = ((x - c) + i gamma) / (sigma sqrt 2)`, `sigma = FWHM_G / (2 sqrt(2 ln 2))`,
  `gamma = FWHM_L / 2`; pseudo-Voigt `eta*L + (1-eta)*G` for K=0 calibration. The fitted model
  is `Voigt ⊗ DHO + elastic Voigt + flat BG` (`convolute_voigt_dho`). Degenerate-limit rule
  (§2): Gaussian and Lorentzian are the pinned `FWHM_L = 0` / `FWHM_G = 0` members of the Voigt
  family.

**Selection (§2)**

- Evidence: `Z_s = ∫ L(theta) pi(theta) d(theta)` **[def]**; selection posterior
  `P(s) = pi_s Z_s / sum_s' pi_s' Z_s'`, uniform `pi` over admissible structures (ruling H4).
- Laplace rung **[def]**: `ln Z ≈ ln L(th^) + ln pi(th^) + (k/2) ln 2pi - (1/2) ln det H`,
  `H` the Hessian of `-ln(L pi)` at the optimum over every free parameter, amplitudes included
  (the fit profiles the amplitudes out, §1.1, but `H` is the joint one) — the Gaussian-bump
  integral; refused on any
  boundary-pinned fit (escalate to nested sampling). Dominance prune: a deferred candidate whose
  evidence *ceiling* sits ≥ 30 nats below a trusted leader is recorded `dominated`, unsampled (the
  pre-ladder lane; the count ladder does not prune, §2.2).
- Diagnostics lane: `AIC = C + 2k`; Akaike weight `w_s = exp(-Delta_s/2) / sum exp(-Delta/2)`.
- Soft dispersion prior (MAP, §5): the Gaussian belief `(prior_omega, prior_sigma)` enters the
  minimized cost once as its `-2 ln` density (`(w0 - prior_omega)^2 / prior_sigma^2` up to the
  convention constant); `cost` vs `posterior_cost` keeps the data-only value separate. **[def —
  exact factor: `analyze/prior.py`]**

**Certification (§§3–4)**

- HESSE: `sigma^2` from the inverse Hessian of the cost at the minimum (the `ΔC = 1` parabola).
  Never certifies. Profile: the `ΔC = 1` crossings of the profiled cost (asymmetric allowed).
- Bootstrap (certifying): M refits of Poisson redraws of the best-fit model; the interval is the
  central 68% of the refit distribution. Coverage is *measured*: with N = 200 truth-scored scans
  per cell, binomial `SE = sqrt(p(1-p)/N) ≈ 0.033` at `p = 0.68`.
- Pooled-master homogeneity gate (§4.1): seeded parametric-bootstrap Poisson **deviance** test,
  `alpha = 0.01`, masks never removes.

**Steering currency (§5 of `design/steering.md`, `design/eig.md`)**

- Assignment honesty meter: Shannon entropy `H = - sum_b p_b ln p_b` over branch weights;
  `H → ln 2` is a coin flip — report ambiguity, never bank the prior.
- Forward model (exposure): `E[n_i] = I_i(theta) * (E_req * r) / f_ref` — requested per-point
  exposure `E_req`, `r` = `request_to_exposure_factor` (request unit → exposure unit; 1.0 on
  TAVI), `f_ref` = `fit_reference_exposure` (the amplitude basis, persisted from the fit). The
  per-bin ledger `ratio_i = sum(M_i) / (n_members * f_ref)` carries heterogeneous illumination
  exactly.
- Expected information gain **[def]**: `EIG(a) = H[QoI] - E_(data ~ predictive)[ H[QoI | data] ]`
  — the mutual information between the QoI (ω₀ bin + identity, targeted lane) and the data the
  action would buy, computed over the model-weighted predictive ensemble; ranked per predicted
  cost. Scan-window decision (ruling O43): the sign of the per-executed-point information delta
  selects the candidate; within the two-sided band `|Delta| < 3 * SE_paired`, or on
  partition-offset disagreement, no preference is expressed and the smaller purchase wins.
